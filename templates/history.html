History of Japanese Writing

The Japanese writing systems Hiragana and Katakana were not invented by a single person. They developed gradually from Chinese characters (Kanji) during the 8th–10th centuries in Japan.

Hiragana evolved from the cursive, flowing style of writing Kanji. It became widely used by women during the Heian period, especially in literature such as The Tale of Genji.

Katakana was created by Buddhist monks who simplified parts of Kanji into shorthand symbols used for reading and annotation.

Together, Hiragana and Katakana became the two phonetic syllabaries used in modern Japanese writing.
